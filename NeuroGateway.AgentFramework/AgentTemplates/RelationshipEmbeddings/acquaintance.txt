The acquaintance relationship represents the minimal biochemical engagement state of social interaction — a neurochemical profile barely distinguishable from baseline solitary functioning with only transient perturbations during direct encounter. The prefrontal cortex maintains default social-monitoring activity through low-level dopamine D2 receptor tonic signaling in the medial prefrontal cortex, sufficient for basic theory-of-mind processing and social norm compliance but without the sustained engagement seen in any deeper relationship type. Serotonergic transmission through 5-HT1A receptors in the raphe nuclei holds steady at resting baseline — there is insufficient social investment to perturb the serotonin system in either positive (bonding-related elevation) or negative (conflict-related suppression) directions. Amygdala activation is minimal and habituated, with initial stranger-threat processing through the basolateral amygdala having extinguished through repeated non-threatening exposure, but without progressing to the safety-signaling amygdala suppression characteristic of trusted relationships. Norepinephrine remains at tonic baseline through alpha-2A autoreceptor regulation in the locus coeruleus, with no phasic bursts of arousal or attention allocation beyond standard social awareness. GABAergic and glutamatergic systems operate at equilibrium — the neural circuitry shows neither the anxiolytic dampening of trusted bonds nor the excitatory potentiation of significant relationships.

The hormonal landscape of acquaintanceship approaches endocrine neutrality. Cortisol maintains normal diurnal rhythm with no measurable perturbation from acquaintance interactions — the HPA axis does not recruit because the social stakes are insufficient to activate hypothalamic CRH release beyond momentary, sub-threshold fluctuations. Oxytocin remains at basal secretion levels from the posterior pituitary with no socially-driven pulses — the paraventricular nucleus is not recruited because the interaction lacks the trust, touch, or emotional vulnerability that triggers oxytocin release. Testosterone shows zero deviation from individual baseline, reflecting the absence of competition, mate-evaluation, or status-challenge dynamics. DHEA and cortisol maintain their standard ratio without perturbation. Progesterone and estrogen follow endogenous cycles uninfluenced by acquaintance interaction. Thyroid function is unaffected — the metabolic demands of acquaintance-level social processing are trivially low compared to the sustained cognitive or emotional engagement of meaningful relationships. Prolactin maintains baseline, reflecting the absence of any bonding, nurturing, or post-intimacy biochemistry.

Peptide engagement during acquaintance interaction is at the functional floor of social neurochemistry. Oxytocin and vasopressin operate at constitutive secretion levels — V1a receptor circuits in the ventral pallidum and lateral septum show no social-bonding or territorial-protective activation, as the acquaintance has not been incorporated into any attachment or in-group schema. Beta-endorphin release through the mu-opioid system is negligible — there is insufficient positive social exchange to activate the endorphin-mediated bonding that characterizes even casual friendship. Neuropeptide Y maintains standard prefrontal anxiolytic tone without social enhancement or depletion. Dynorphin and substance P remain at baseline — the kappa-opioid dysphoria and NK1-mediated emotional pain systems are completely disengaged because no attachment exists to generate loss-related or conflict-related distress. Orexin operates at standard wakefulness levels without the social-arousal enhancement seen in more engaging relationships. The acquaintance profile is defined by what is absent rather than what is present: it is the neurochemical control condition against which all other relationship types' biochemical signatures become measurable, serving as the zero-point calibration for embedding-based relationship differentiation.
